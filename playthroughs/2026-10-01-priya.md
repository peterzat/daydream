# Playthrough, 2026-10-01: Priya

## 1. Overall impression

A warm, well-written little game. The watercolor banners, the "How to Dream" guide inside the front cover (shot 5), and a painted portrait of my character that appeared beside my name after I described myself (shot 14) all made it feel cozy and cared for. The writing is the best part: Bell counting visitors on sooty fingers, Tock's "purr that keeps perfect time", the stray minute that was "a morning stretch so enormous it ended in a small squeak" (shot 120). As someone who has never played a text adventure, I found the typed box ("speak, friend...") forgiving. It understood "give the escapement gear to Tace" and "open the clock case with the key" on the first try.

The main friction was the interface, not the game. The story text lives in a small scrolling column, so new replies often appeared below the fold and I had to scroll inside it after almost every action (shots 13-15). That made each step cost about twice as many actions as it should have.

## 2. Did I solve it?

Yes, as far as the first-day story goes. The goal on arrival was clear from the repair ledger (shot 12-15): the great clock stopped when its escapement gear rolled away south toward the well-court. Whoever brings it to Tace in the loft earns the case-key. I found the gear in the Mossy Well-Court (shot 37), gave it to Tace and got the key, then opened the clock case. The clock started ticking and the header changed from "TIME STANDS STILL" to "DAY 1 · DUSK" (shot 84), which is how I know it was the main goal. Afterwards, "what now?" sent me to Tace for "the first winding". I wound my own keeper's clock (shot ~104), visited Mott, collected a stray minute, and planted my dreamseed.

Left unfinished: the little brass clock marked "W" that Tace will not let me wind (a side mystery), the orchard, the balcony, the cellar, Lamplight Lane, and Quill's topics. I stopped because my command budget was nearly used up, not because I was stuck.

## 3. Surprises (good and bad)

Good:
- My portrait was painted from my few words of description (shot 14).
- Starting the clock had a real sense of payoff: the tower "seems to breathe out", and the day/time label appears (shot 84).
- Planting a dreamseed made a real new room with generated prose and a painted banner ("painting..." placeholder in shot 139, finished art in shot 145). The garden reacted too: "a lantern comes alight early" in the square.
- The Ledger of Returned Hours already had my name signed in it ("Priya (here now)", shot 19).

Bad:
- Tag numbers and positions shift whenever the column scrolls. My click meant for "up" sent me east to the square instead (shot 24).
- The puzzle chain was very easy. The gear was lying in plain sight one room after Bell said where it was.
- In my own Reading Nook, "read the paper book" gave "You can't read the paper book." That was a letdown in a room built around books.
- Typing "ask Mott about the tin" got the reply for "a hush" instead, and handed me the hush (shot 120). Clicking the "the tin" chip worked properly.

## 4. Dead ends and blockers

No bail-out.
- The worn repair ledger was described in the room prose but not listed under Around You (shot 9). Typing "read the repair ledger" got me past this.
- My first scroll on the tagged scrollbar did nothing (shot 10). Scrolling with the pointer over the text column worked.
- The little brass clock "W": "wind the little brass clock" got "Not that one, friend" (shot ~66), and asking Tace about it gets a deflection. I did not crack this one and did not have budget to pursue it.
- My first dreamseed description was too long ("The seed trembles under so many words; hold a smaller vision."). A shorter one worked straight away.

## 5. Suggestions for improvement (clarity, gameplay)

- Auto-scroll the story column to the newest reply, or make it taller. This was the single biggest annoyance.
- List every item the prose mentions under Around You (the repair ledger in the Clocktower).
- When "what now?" tells me to ask about a topic ("the first winding"), add that topic as a chip on that person.
- Do not offer topics (like "a hush") before the player has heard of them, or have Mott mention it first.
- Fix the typed-ask mismatch so "ask Mott about the tin" asks about the tin.
- Tell the player the length limit for a dreamseed vision up front, for example "a few words (under ten)".
- Let readable-looking objects in player-made rooms be read, even with a generic line.
- Tace's "Bell will want to meet you" should recognize that I had already met Bell.

## 6. The puzzles: difficulty, and were they interesting?

The main puzzle (find the gear, get the key, open the case) was very easy. Every step was pointed out directly by the ledger or by Bell. As an onboarding for a newcomer to text adventures it worked well, and it taught giving, using and opening in a natural order. As a puzzle it was not challenging. The W clock looked like the more interesting thread, a quiet mystery hinted at by Tace's evasions, and I would have liked more time for it.

## 7. Making my mark

I found out I could make something when "Plant" appeared as a verb after I picked up the dreamseed (shot 86). The guide's mention of keepsakes hinted at it too. In the Pendulum Garden I typed "plant the dreamseed" and the game showed the format: "plant dreamseed: a place you'd like to find". My first, long vision was rejected as too many words. "a willow reading nook with a book-swap shelf" worked. A new exit south opened to "The Willow Reading Nook", with its own prose (willow roof, wicker chair, shelf of worn paper books) and, a few seconds later, a watercolor banner (shots 139, 145). I left the warm brass cog from the clock case there for whoever comes next. Earlier I also shaped my character's look on the setup screen, and that turned into a portrait. Making a room was the highlight of the session and felt like a real contribution to a shared village. The only letdown was that the books in my nook could not be read.
