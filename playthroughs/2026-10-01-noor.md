# Playthrough, 2026-10-01: Noor

## 1. Overall impression

This was a lovely evening. From the first screen (shot 1) the watercolor village and the "storybook" framing set a calm, warm mood, and the game kept that mood almost the whole way through. I have never played a text adventure, and I was worried I would be staring at a blank prompt. I wasn't. The verb buttons, the underlined words in the prose and the topic chips beside each person always gave me something obvious to try. When I typed in my own words, the game usually understood me (shots 19, 43, 60, 74, 81).

Moment to moment it felt like reading a gentle picture book that answered back. Highlights:
- My portrait was painted from my three-word description (shot 10).
- The Ledger already had my name on its last page (shot 13).
- The great clock took its first tick and the header changed from "TIME STANDS STILL" to "DAY 1 · DUSK" (shot 46).
- Petting Tock got me "a purr that keeps perfect time" (shot 24).
- The "snail minute" and the "sunbreak minute" (shots 57, 78).
- The little runaway birthday nap who was "Not sleepy... Very sleepy" (shot 70), and the ending where "a boy named Pim finally sleeps, with a red kite leaning by his bed" (shot 91).

The writing is the star. The low points were small: a few robotic parser seams, a cramped side panel, and the letter feature, which I could not use.

## 2. Did I solve it?

I think the opening goal was to get the stopped great clock going again. The repair ledger (shot 15) spells it out: find the escapement gear, bring it to Tace, earn the case-key. I did all of that and opened the case (shot 46). Then came a second story: return Pim's lost nap. I fetched a hush from Mott (shot 76) and a pendulum for Tace to build a lullaby clock (shot 87), then wound it beside the nap (shot 91). I also wound my own keeper's clock (shot 64).

I know I finished both because the Ledger of Returned Hours changed. It went from "No hour has been returned yet" (shot 13) to "The great clock was mended by Noor... Day 1: Noor sang Pim's missing nap home with a mended lullaby clock" (shot 126). I did not explore the cellar, the Waiting House, the bridge or the Hill of Long Shadows, so there is surely more.

## 3. Surprises (good and bad)

Good:
- My painted portrait (shot 10) and the room picture painted from what I planted (shots 98-99).
- Mistakes get answered in character. When I tried to wind the wrong clock, Tace said "Those are resting, friend. Yours is the one to wind: the small clock in your pocket" (shot 62).
- The village notices what I did. Bell heard my clock and introduced the lanterns by name (shot 79). Bell's evening count says "one dreamer, Noor, came through today" (shot 66). The Ledger records my deeds (shot 126).
- Topics beside each person grow with what I have learned (shot 27).
- Choosing a verb dims the things it cannot apply to (shot 45). Very helpful.
- The planted tree kept my detail about ticking "a half-beat apart" (shot 103).

Bad:
- "pick up the gear and wipe the moss off it" gave "You don't see the wipe the moss off it here." (shot 33). This broke the storybook voice right after the guide promised "the dream is good at guessing".
- The keepsake icons look shuffled (shot 54). The case key has a leaf icon, the dreamseed has a gear, and the brass cog has a sun.
- After I planted my tree, I told Quill about it. My line printed as "Noor: ..." with no reply, even after waiting (shots 110-111). It felt like talking to a wall at my proudest moment.
- A small logic itch: I handed the gear to Tace upstairs, then opened the case downstairs and the clock simply worked (shots 43, 46). Who put the gear back in?

## 4. Dead ends and blockers

I did not bail out. I stopped at move 128, after leaving the dream and signing out, because both stories had ended and I was content.

- **Room text cut off.** The room text is cut off behind "MORE", and scrolling over it did nothing (shot 10). Clicking "MORE" worked (shot 11).
- **Crowded side panel.** Once Tock followed me into the loft, the "ask Tace / ask Tock / around you / you carry" links overlapped Tock's name and hid the topic chips (shot 58). I got around it by typing my question instead (shot 60).
- **Half-hidden stray minute.** The stray minute in the Clocktower was half-hidden at the top of the scrolled panel (shot 55). I clicked its faded text directly.
- **Letters (not resolved).** The guide says I can "leave them a letter to find later (write to Wren: ...)". At the post office, "write to the next dreamer: ..." was refused: "No dreamer here goes by the next dreamer" (shot 119). "write to Wren" was refused too: "No dreamer here goes by Wren" (shot 120). I never saw an "also dreaming" list or met another player, so I had no real name to write to. I gave up on letters.
- **Misclick.** I picked "Use" instead of "Read" once, because the number tags sit between the buttons (shot 124). That one was my fault.

## 5. Suggestions for improvement (clarity, gameplay)

- Let the room text scroll with the mouse wheel, or show more of it by default. Having to click "MORE" in almost every room was a small but constant friction.
- Give the side panel more room once there are two people present, so the section links don't sit on top of names and topics (shot 58).
- Fix the keepsake icons (shot 54).
- Soften the parser fallback. When half of a command is not understood, quietly drop it or answer in the storybook voice, instead of "You don't see the wipe the moss off it here" (shot 33).
- When I speak a person's name at the start of a line ("Quill, I planted..."), treat it as speech to that person and give some reply, even a nod (shot 110).
- Letters. Either use a real dreamer's name in the guide's example, or let a player write to "whoever comes next" (Fen's own line, "Every letter wants reading by someone, even if it's not the someone on the front", practically invites it, shot 117). Or have Fen suggest who can receive post.
- Consider having the Ledger also note the things a player plants. My saffron tree is not mentioned in it (shot 126).
- The "How to Dream" guide (shot 5) is helpful but dense for a first screen. Maybe show it in two short pages, or surface each tip the first time it becomes relevant.

## 6. The puzzles: difficulty, and were they interesting?

Both puzzles were easy, and for a cozy game that felt right.

The clock puzzle was over-signposted. The repair ledger, Tace and Bell all told me "south, the well-court" (shots 15, 21, 30). Then the gear was simply lying in the open (shot 31). I expected at least a little searching.

The nap puzzle was the better one. It had two steps that made sense in the fiction:
- A hush quiets the nap but "it needs something slow to fall asleep to" (shot 81).
- A pendulum becomes a lullaby clock (shot 87).

Bell gave two leads at once (shot 74), which let me feel I was choosing where to go. Neither puzzle was hard, but both were interesting because the solutions were poetic rather than mechanical.

## 7. Making my mark

Three things:

1. **My dreamer.** At the start I described myself as "a long saffron scarf, ink-stained fingers, a sleepy smile" (shot 8). A watercolor portrait of a woman in a saffron scarf appeared beside my name (shot 10). It is shown everywhere I go. Lovely.

2. **Planting a dreamseed.** I found the seed after mending the clock (shot 48), and taking it revealed a "Plant" verb (shot 52). In the Pendulum Garden I chose Plant and then the dreamseed. The line then asked "What do you see growing there?" (shot 96). I wrote: "a small saffron-colored tree whose seed pods tick softly in the wind, each one a half-beat apart". The game answered: "The dreamseed takes root, and the dream makes room. A new way opens to the south: The Saffron Tick Tree... it was your hands that made the room" (shot 97). A new exit appeared. The room had its own prose and a picture painted on arrival (shots 98-99). It also had objects that echoed my words, like a ticking seed pod "half a slow second apart from the others" (shot 103). This was the best moment of the whole session. It felt like I had really added a place to someone else's village. One letdown: when I told Quill about it, nobody answered (shot 111).

3. **A letter.** I tried to leave a letter for the next visitor, recommending Tock and my tree. Fen refused it twice because I had no real dreamer's name (shots 119-120). So this one did not work.

## 8. Knowledge discrepancies

- Shot 5: the "How to Dream" guide names "Wren" and "the cog" ("give the cog to Wren") before I have met either. The cog later turned out to be real (shot 46), but Wren is not a dreamer at all (shot 120), so the example points at someone who does not exist.
- Shots 66-67: "Pim's Nap" appears as present and "Sorrel" is named in the narration with no prior mention. This is told as a live event, so it is mostly fine, but Sorrel is never shown as present or explained.
- Shot 91: Bell's topic list offers "Linden", a name nobody had mentioned to me. Asking explained it (Linden keeps the Waiting House, shot 114), but the topic came before any introduction.
- Opposite case, shots 5 and 119-120: the guide says I can write to other dreamers listed under "also dreaming", but no such list or other dreamer was ever shown. So I could not act on a feature the game told me about.
