# Playthrough, 2026-10-01: Silas

## 1. Overall impression

This was a warm, lovely evening. It felt like reading a picture book that answers back. The watercolor art (login page, shot 1; every room after that) and the writing set the mood straight away, and the mood held for the whole session. Several lines stopped me for a second: the cat's "purr that keeps perfect time" (shot 34), the garden that "ticks once, all together, like a great clock remembering itself" (shot 51), Tace saying "I keep checking, the way you check a sleeping child is breathing" (shot 79), and Bell counting "one dreamer, Silas, came through today" at the last lantern pole (shot 85).

As someone who had never played a text adventure, I was a little overwhelmed by the "How to Dream" guide (shot 5). It is clear, but it explains eight things at once before I had touched anything. Once I was in, the verb-then-thing pattern (click Read, then click the ledger) clicked within two moves (shots 11 to 12). Faded options show what a verb can't apply to, which is a quiet, good hint.

Moment to moment I went from charmed, to curious (the evasive clockmaker, the W on the little clock), to having a real purpose (find the gear), to a proper payoff when the clock started and the subtitle changed from "TIME STANDS STILL" to "DAY 1 · DUSK" (shot 67). The second half was looser and had more friction: a hidden response after a give, a layout shift that made me misclick, and a planting prompt that turned down my words twice.

## 2. Did I solve it?

I think the main goal was to mend the great clock so time starts again in the village. I did that. I found the missing escapement gear in the ferns at the well-court (shot 48), gave it to Tace and got the case key back (shot 64), opened the clock case and set the gear (shot 67). The Ledger of Returned Hours then read "The great clock was mended by Silas, and time began again in the village" (shot 74), so I'm confident that part is done.

After that a second thread opened up. A "birthday nap too excited to be taken" (Pim's Nap) was wandering the square (shot 89). I got a hush from Mott and gave it to the nap, which made it "drowsy" (shot 99). Tace then said a lullaby clock needs a "spare brass pendulum from the nail by the window, or from wherever it has wandered" (shot 106). I didn't find that pendulum before my budget ran low, so this thread is unfinished. I also never visited the cellar (down from the clocktower), Lamplight Lane (east of the square), or the orchard (east of the garden).

## 3. Surprises (good and bad)

Good:
- My name was already in the Ledger as a keeper, "Silas (here now)" (shot 12), and after I fixed the clock it named me as the mender (shot 74). That felt like being part of the place.
- The W scratched on the little brass clock (shot 24) paid off much later, when Tace said "Wend made a lullaby clock once" (shot 106). I worked out quietly that Wend was who Tace had lost. Nobody spelled it out. That was the best writing in the game.
- Characters' moods change: Tace went from "wistful" to "gladdened" (shot 75), and the nap from "restless" to "drowsy" (shot 99).
- The clock's first tick changed the subtitle, and the square at dusk had changed (lanterns lit, a cat appearing between ticks) (shots 67, 85).
- I got a clock of my own to wind, "a half-beat apart from every other clock" (shot 83).
- Planting a dreamseed made a whole new room from my words, with its own painted picture (shots 122 to 124).
- The Give prompt "give escapement gear to... (touch someone, or give again to change your mind)" (shot 63) was exactly the help I needed at that moment.

Bad:
- When I gave the hush to the nap (shot 98), nothing visible changed. The response had been written above where the story column was scrolled, and I only found it by scrolling (shot 99).
- The verb row wrapped onto two lines in the loft (shot 76), the buttons shifted, and I clicked Use instead of Talk.
- Clicking the word "pendulums" in Quill's speech examined "the trellises" instead (shot 56).
- The dreamseed prompt refused my first vision for being too long (shot 116) without having said there was a limit, then refused an 11-word one with "nothing of that kind grows in this dream" and no hint why (shot 119).
- The painted picture of my flower shows white petals, though I asked for pale blue and the room text says "dusk blue" (shots 122 to 124).
- The paper time label in my new room holds "a handwritten note in careful script" (shot 125), but there's no way to read it. Typing "read the note" just repeated the description (shot 127).

## 4. Dead ends and blockers

I didn't bail out. I stopped because my move budget was nearly spent.

- The locked clock case (shot 16). I got past it the intended way, through Tace and Bell. Not really a blocker, more the hook.
- Tace wouldn't let me wind the little brass clock ("Not that one, friend", shot 26), wouldn't say who W was (shot 31), and said to ask about the frost "on a warmer day" (shot 40). These felt like closed doors, but kind ones, and the story did open them later.
- Giving to Tace (shot 61): I clicked Give, Tace greyed out, and for a moment I didn't understand that I had to pick the item first. Opening my carried items fixed it (shots 62 to 63).
- The hush response hidden off-screen (shot 98). I scrolled to find it (shot 99).
- Winding my own clock near the nap did nothing for it (shot 102). Asking Tace in plain words got me the real hint (shot 106).
- The spare brass pendulum. I went to the Pendulum Garden thinking of the "littlest pendulum", but it is raindrop-sized and Take is greyed out (shots 110 to 112). Its tag was also hard to hit, so I clicked the word directly. I didn't find the right pendulum. Unresolved.
- Planting: two refusals before the third try worked (shots 116, 119, 122). I got through by guessing plainer words.
- The handwritten note on the paper time label can't be read (shots 125, 127). Unresolved.

## 5. Suggestions for improvement (clarity, gameplay)

- After any action, scroll the story column so the newest response is in view (shot 98).
- Keep the verb row a stable size, or keep the order fixed when verbs are added, so buttons don't move under the cursor (shot 76).
- Show the dreamseed word limit in the prompt itself (for example "a dozen words or so"). When a vision is refused, give a hint of what kind of thing can grow (shots 116, 119).
- Make the painting match the colors the player typed (shot 124).
- Either show the words of the handwritten note in a planted room, or don't describe it as holding writing (shot 125).
- Introduce the guide a little at a time, or let the first room teach one verb before the whole guide appears (shot 5).
- Topics that appear before anyone mentions them ("the gear", "Linden", "the first winding") leak the story a bit early. Let them appear after the line that introduces them.
- Make sure clicking a word in prose acts on that word (pendulums vs trellises, shot 56).

## 6. The puzzles: difficulty, and were they interesting?

The clock puzzle was easy but satisfying, and right for a cozy game. Every step was signposted by a person: Tace pointed to Bell, Bell pointed to the well-court, the prose there said "something small among them catches the light", and examining the ferns found the gear. What made it interesting wasn't difficulty. It was that each step came with a small piece of a person. Being handed the key "by custom the one who carries it home sets it in" (shot 64) made the solve feel like something given to me, not something I worked out.

The nap puzzle had a lovely premise. The hush step was too easy (Mott just handed it over, shot 91). The pendulum step was where I got stuck, mainly because the obvious candidate (the littlest pendulum) couldn't be taken and nothing told me why. Hiding the lullaby-clock hint behind a topic I had to type out myself (Bell said to ask Tace, but no chip offered it, shot 104) was a little harsh for a newcomer. Typing in plain words did work well, though.

## 7. Making my mark

Several things changed because of me:
- Character creation: I described myself as "tall and stooped, grey wool scarf, ink on his fingers" (shot 8), and a matching painted portrait of an older man in a cap appeared (shot 10). That was a nice moment.
- Mending the clock got my name into the Ledger of Returned Hours permanently (shot 74). Bell also counts me among the day's dreamers (shot 85).
- Planting. The dreamseed dropped out of the clock as a thank-you (shot 68), and a Plant verb appeared once I had it (shot 70). I chose the Pendulum Garden because it was the prettiest spot. The game asked "What do you see growing there?" (shot 115), which was a delight: I got to write part of the world. My first try, a lantern-flower humming half a beat slow in memory of Wend, was too long (shot 116). My second, shorter version was turned down with no reason given (shot 119), which deflated me, since it was the one I cared about. The third, "a small pale-blue flower with a brass-colored center," took root. The dream "made room" for a new place to the south, "The Blue-Centered Bloom", and a lantern in the square lit early "the way a house does when someone dear is coming home" (shot 122). Walking into a room I had named, and watching its picture get painted (shots 123 to 124), was the high point of the session.

What I think: this is a wonderful mechanic and the in-world wording of the refusals is gentle. But being refused twice, the second time for no reason I could see, made it feel like guessing a password. I ended up planting something blander than what I wanted. I didn't try writing a letter to another dreamer, because the guide's example (Wren) was the only name I had and I hadn't met any other dreamers.
