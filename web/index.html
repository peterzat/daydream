    How to Dream

    a small guide, tucked inside the front cover

    speaking

    the line at the foot of the page — speak, friend... — hears plain words. say hello to whoever is near, ask about what you see, or describe what you’d like to try. the dream is good at guessing.

    doing

    the what you might do ribbon holds your verbs. touch one to stage it, then touch a thing or a friend in the margin to act on it. touching something with nothing staged simply looks closer.

    asking

    under here with you, each person lists what you could ask them about. touch a topic to ask. the village remembers what you do, and so do its people.

    wandering

    the ways from here compass at the foot of the page lists every path out of a room. touch one to wander.

    the satchel

    open the satchel lays out your keepsakes — the things you carry, and in time the story of your dreams. stray minutes glint about the village, a few each day just for you; pick them up and they settle into your book.

    waking

    leave the dream rests your toon safely and returns you to the choosing page; switch toon takes up another. your keepsakes and your book stay with you; things that belong to the village find their way home while you sleep.
