      How to Dream

      a small guide, tucked inside the front cover

      speaking

      the line at the foot of the page — speak, friend... — hears plain words. say hello to whoever is near, ask about what you see, or describe what you’d like to try. the dream is good at guessing.

      doing

      the what you might do ribbon holds what you can do. touch one, then touch whoever or whatever you mean in the margin. touching something with nothing chosen simply looks closer.

      asking

      under here with you, each person lists what you could ask them about. touch a topic to ask. {{place}} remembers what you do, and so do its people.

      wandering

      the ways from here compass at the foot of the page lists every path out of a room. touch one to wander.

      the satchel

      open the satchel lays out your keepsakes — the things you carry, the threads you’re following, and in time the story of your dreams (or just ask what now?). stray minutes glint about {{place}}, a few each day just for you; pick them up and they settle into your book.

      waking

      leave the dream lets your dreamer rest, and you can step back in whenever you like. touch your own name, under you, for your dreamer and your account. your keepsakes and your book stay with you; things that belong to {{place}} find their way home while you sleep.
