# Playtest: the veteran (Halloran)

Persona: a seasoned MMORPG player (raids, guilds, auction houses, roleplay servers) and a serious reader and writer. Arrived alone in the evening after two housemates, Vex (plays fast) and Wren (reads everything), had played earlier the same day. Judged the world by what it could tell me about them, what I could do for them while they were away, and whether it gives a reason to log in daily.

Dreamer: Halloran, "a tall woman with grey at her temples, reading glasses pushed up into her hair, a wool coat that has seen many winters, and a worn leather satchel stuffed with notebooks."

Session: 2026-09-28, dusk, day 1 of the village. About 105 commands over roughly 40 minutes, at ten seconds or more between commands. The village clock read "day 1, dusk" from the first line to the last; no dusk passed while I played.

## Session summary

Woke in the Clocktower and read both books before touching anything: the Ledger of Returned Hours (two deeds, both credited to Wren and Vex by name) and the repair ledger (Tace's note to "new keepers"). Went up to Tace, asked about the first winding, was handed a small clock and wound it. Went down and east; Bell came off the ladder saying she could hear my clock from the square. Mott in the Old Workshop gave me the Book of Stray Minutes and my first minute.

Then a full circuit. North of the tower: the Bowl of Turned Time, a grown room with a spent husk in it. South of the square: the Mossy Well-Court, where a polished brass hand lay on the ground that did not belong there; above it the Waiting Bell Loft, another grown room. South again: the Pendulum Garden (Quill) and the Orchard of Evenings. East: Lamplight Lane, the Little Post Office (Fen), where I wrote a letter each to Wren and to Vex and left the brass hand in the dead-letter drawer for Vex; the Waiting House (Linden) and the Lamp House above it; the Lantern Bridge, the Dusk Road (Sorrel), the Hill of Long Shadows (sat on the bench, waited), the Slow River's Edge. Back in the tower a third dreamer, Peek, stood dozing. Down to the Hour Cellar (Umber) and north into the Warmth of Unsent Words, a third grown room holding a live dreamseed. Carried it back to the cellar and planted it with "a reading nook where three housemates leave bookmarks in each other's books"; the Quiet Bookmark Shelf grew to the east with three armchairs in it. Took the linen bookmark, climbed to the Winding Balcony to look through the telescope, then walked the bookmark to the post office and left it in the drawer for Wren. Rang Fen's bell and left.

Threads found: Bell (meet her), Mott (the book), both closed within ten minutes; afterwards "what now" said nothing was tugging at my sleeve. Book: 4/172, all four on the Morning Minutes page. Nothing in the world sent me to a guest still waiting to go home; Wren and Vex had already done the great clock and Pim's nap.

## Rubric

1. **Surprise: 4.** Bell coming down the ladder two rungs at a time because she could hear the clock I had wound thirty seconds earlier in another building. The planted seed producing three armchairs, one for each of us, without my having said "three chairs". `eat the evening fruit` refusing me with "far too lovely to eat". Tock leaning into my hand "for exactly one tick".
2. **Consequence: 3.** Winding the clock changed how the next villager greeted me. Planting the seed opened a real room and the cellar's exit line now reads "grown from Halloran's dreamseed". Two letters were filed and two objects sit in the drawer. But nothing I did came back to me from a villager later: Fen did not mention the letters on my second visit, the Ledger did not record the planting, and the two starter threads closed without anyone noting it.
3. **Being remembered: 2.** Quill called me "dear Halloran" once and Bell knew I was "Tace's new keeper" by the sound of my clock. Nobody remembered a conversation on a return visit, and, the point of the session, nobody remembered Wren and Vex as people. Bell "inferred" them from the Ledger ("must have been here then"); Quill and Sorrel invented their whereabouts; Mott invented having kept their minutes. The Ledger remembers deeds. The villagers do not remember dreamers.
4. **Reason to return: 3.** The Book's daily minutes and the post office are a real coffee-break loop, and I want to know whether my housemates find what I left. The doubt is whether the world will tell me: there is no inbox check, no "Wren read your letter", and the improvised answers about absent players are unreliable enough that I would stop asking.

## Defects

Each with the exact command, what happened, what I expected.

1. **An incidental word steals a real question (five times).** `talk to Tace: Two friends of mine were here earlier today, Wren and Vex. The ledger downstairs says they mended the great clock. What were they like when they came through?` produced the canned line for the topic "the great clock" ("Hear that? Every tick is the village keeping a promise"). `talk to Bell: I live with two people who were here before me today, Wren and Vex. Did you meet them? What did they do in your square?` produced the "the square" topic ("Best little square in the dream!"). `talk to Fen: Has anything come in addressed to Halloran? Or did Wren or Vex leave a letter here for each other today? I would carry it if you let me.` produced "the letters" ("Written, sealed, never sent"). `talk to Quill: I looked. There was nobody in the orchard but the fruit. Why did you tell me they were there?` produced "the orchard". `talk to Umber: You label things, so you notice who comes and goes. Two friends of mine, Wren and Vex, were here before me today. What did they do down here, if anything?` produced "the Hour Cellar" ("Under the tower. Cool, dry, one patient lantern"). Expected: an answer to the question I asked, with the topic line as a follow-on at most. For a player who types full sentences this is the defect that shapes every conversation; after the third time I started censoring my own vocabulary.
2. **A question that got no answer at all.** `talk to Tace: Do you remember Wren and Vex? They are friends of mine. I want to know what they did here.` Nothing came back, not then, not in the `look` ten seconds later, not in any later call. Expected a reply or an in-world "Tace does not answer".
3. **Improvised replies arrive after I have moved on.** `talk to Bell: Never mind the geography. Wren and Vex are my housemates. Did they come through here earlier? What were they like?` was answered about 25 seconds later, printed under "(meanwhile)" during my next command. Same for Mott (about 20 seconds). A player at a normal pace reads the answer to the previous question while doing something else, or leaves the room before it comes.
4. **Villagers invent where my friends are.** `talk to Quill: Forgive me, I asked badly. Two friends, Wren and Vex. Were they here today? Did they speak with you?` gave "they might be resting in the Orchard of Evenings right now." The orchard was empty. `talk to Sorrel: I am Halloran. Two people I live with, Wren and Vex, were in the village before me today. Did you see them out here with your net?` gave "They are in good hands now, resting in the Waiting House where hours find their home." I had left the Waiting House ten minutes earlier with nobody in it but Linden. Two false errands in one evening. Expected honest not-knowing ("I have not seen them, but the Ledger has their names").
5. **Mott's improvised reply.** `talk to Mott: You keep found things. Did anyone leave something here today, or ask you to hold something for a friend? I am looking for word of Wren and Vex.` gave "No one asked to hold anything for a friend today, little one," then "I have kept the stray minutes they left behind." "Little one" to a grey-templed woman is a tone break; the kept minutes are an invented fact (my own minutes were private to me, so theirs were to them).
6. **The key board cannot be read.** `examine the board of room keys` in the Waiting House: "each hook labeled in chalk with the name of an hour waiting to go home", no names. Linden had just told me "a name in chalk means someone's waiting. A clean slate means someone's gone home." This is the quest board, and it is blank to the reader. Expected the chalk names, and clean hooks for Pim's nap and the great clock's hour.
7. **Disambiguation fails on an exact name, and the click grounds to the wrong object.** In the Warmth of Unsent Words, with both a "dreamseed" and a "spent dreamseed" present: `examine the dreamseed` asked "Which dreamseed do you mean: dreamseed or spent dreamseed?" Then, carrying the live seed, `plant the dreamseed: a reading nook where three housemates leave bookmarks in each other's books` asked the same question. Then `click plant dreamseed --text "..."` answered "You can't plant the spent dreamseed." I had to walk to the cellar to plant. Expected an exact name to win, and a carried plantable seed to beat a husk on the floor.
8. **A bell that says it is not there.** In the Waiting Bell Loft, with "polished brass bell" listed in the room, `ring the polished brass bell` gave "There's no bell here to ring. The only one you know of sits on the counter of the Little Post Office." Expected either a ring or a line about this bell.
9. **Verbatim repeat.** `write to Vex: ...` produced Fen's letter narration word for word from the Wren letter with the name swapped ("Fen slides a sheet of good paper and a soft pencil across the counter...").
10. **Tense slip.** After I told Bell how each housemate would treat the lanterns, Bell asked "did Wren find a stray minute to keep in their pocket while you were here?" Wren was not with me and I had just arrived.
11. **The three earlier grown rooms do not name their makers.** The cellar's new exit reads "toward the Quiet Bookmark Shelf, grown from Halloran's dreamseed"; the exits to the Bowl of Turned Time, the Waiting Bell Loft and the Warmth of Unsent Words read only "A new way opens...". The spent husks say nothing about who planted them (`examine the spent dreamseed`: "its dreaming is done, and the place around it is what the dreaming became"). The traces I most wanted were the ones the world withheld.
12. **No inbox.** Nothing I tried tells me whether anyone wrote to me: `examine the pigeonholes` lists only the authored labels; `read the letters` is refused (rightly, for others' letters); `talk to Fen: Has anything come in addressed to Halloran?...` got the canned topic (defect 1). Expected Fen to say "nothing for you yet" or "one, from Wren".
13. **The dead-letter drawer works but says nothing about intent.** `put the polished brass hand in the dead-letter drawer` worked and `examine the dead-letter drawer` shows "holds: polished brass hand", but nothing records that it is for Vex; Fen's "I will mark it for Vex" is a nice line with, as far as I can tell, nothing behind it.
14. **"look into the river"** at the Slow River's Edge reprinted the room description. Minor, but "something small and bright turns over under the surface" invites exactly that command.
15. **`ring the brass bell`** in the post office, first and only ring: "Heard you the first time." Small, and it reads as if the line was written for a second ring.
16. **My punctuation was dropped** in the echo of `talk to Fen: ... I would carry it if you let me.` (the trailing period vanished). Cosmetic.
17. **A dozing dreamer cannot be left a note in place.** `say to Peek: Evening. I am Halloran...` gave "Peek is dozing, far off in a dream of their own, and doesn't stir." Correct and well put, but the only way to reach Peek was to walk to the post office. Expected at least a hint ("Fen would take a letter").

## Three best moments

1. Coming east into the square and having Bell come down the ladder two rungs at a time: "You're Tace's new keeper! I can hear your clock from here." One small action in one room reached the next room before I did.
2. Planting a propagated seed I had found in a room one of my housemates grew, and walking east into the Quiet Bookmark Shelf: "Three armchairs sit in a gentle circle, each holding a book open to a page where a bookmark waits." ## Traces of the others

What the world told me about Wren and Vex, and where.

- **The Ledger of Returned Hours (Clocktower).** "The great clock was mended by Wren and Vex together, and time began again in the village." and "Day 1: Wren and Vex sang Pim's missing nap home with a mended lullaby clock." The only place in the village that states plainly what they did, and it credits them together, which told me more about how they played than anything a villager said.
- **The repair ledger (Clocktower).** The later pages say the clock stopped "on the night of the first hard frost and was mended again"; Tace's newest page addresses "new keepers", plural. Their path, described as a custom.
- **The clock case (Clocktower).** "The door stands open, and behind the glass the pendulum sways bright and sure." Their work, visible. The case key is back on the loft workbench.
- **The lullaby clock (Clockmaker's Loft).** Tace, asked about it: "It sang the little nap home. Wend's pattern still works, it seems." Their deed without their names.
- **Three grown rooms, unattributed.** The Bowl of Turned Time north of the tower ("The stone curves upward like a minute hand waiting to be mended"); the Waiting Bell Loft above the well-court ("a thick rope ladder ... waiting for a friend to pull themselves up", a bell that rings "the moment the secret password is spoken", which reads like Vex to me); the Warmth of Unsent Words north of the cellar ("holding every letter that was never sent", with a "folded paper hour" and a "folded paper weight", which reads like Wren). Each holds a spent husk that says nothing about its planter. The Warmth held a live seed, which is how I came to plant next to them.
- **A polished brass hand in the Mossy Well-Court.** Its own description says it lies "curved on the ramp"; the well-court has no ramp. Somebody carried it from a grown room and put it down. I chose to believe it was Vex, moving fast.
- **Bell (Lantern Square).** "Wren and Vex must have been here then, for I hear how the great clock ticks now thanks to them." Derived from the Ledger, framed as inference. Later, riffing on my descriptions: "Vex would have loved the way Pollen hiccups."
- **Quill, Sorrel, Mott.** Invented whereabouts (the Orchard, the Waiting House) and an invented keepsake (their minutes). I learned nothing true from them.
- **Peek (Clocktower).** A third dreamer, "a quiet person in a grey coat with a notebook", dozing. Not one of mine, but the first sign that others come and go.

What I left for them or did for them.

- A letter to Wren and a letter to Vex, filed by Fen ("It'll be here when Wren next comes by. Everything arrives eventually.").
- The polished brass hand, in the dead-letter drawer, with Fen's word that she would "mark it for Vex".
- A room: the Quiet Bookmark Shelf, east of the Hour Cellar, three chairs, the oak bookmark still in it, the exit line naming me.
- The linen bookmark from that room, in the dead-letter drawer for Wren.
- I put the Bowl's spent husk back where I found it after picking it up, on the grounds that it was theirs.

What I wished I could learn or do.

- Which of them grew which room, and when. The engine can do it (it did it for mine).
- Read the chalk names on the key board and see which hooks they wiped clean.
- A last-seen: "Wren was here at dusk." Even "someone was here an hour ago" would do.
- Address a thing to a person rather than to a drawer, and have the world remember the addressing.
- Know whether my letters will be announced to them, and whether I will be told when they are read.
- Have a villager remember a conversation with them, not only a deed from a book.
- Find something they dropped on purpose for me. If they did, I did not find it, and the world gave me no way to know.

## As a social game

The systematic pass, in the voice of someone who has lived in a dozen of these.

- **Who's online.** Partial. Co-located dreamers appear under "Here with you" and speaking to a disconnected one gets "is dozing ... and doesn't stir", which is a good in-fiction presence state. There is no list of who is awake anywhere in the village and no last-seen. I did not try a bare `who`; nothing suggested it existed. Wanted here: yes, quietly. A line from Bell at dusk, "two dreamers walked through today", would be enough.
- **Friends list.** None. Wanted, in the smallest possible form: the Ledger already knows my housemates as a pair; let the game know them as mine.
- **Mail.** Yes, and it is the best-fitting system in the village: a post office, a clerk, `write to X: ...`, filed by name. Missing the other half: no inbox, no delivery notice, no "read" state. In a cozy game the inbox is the daily reason to log in, so this matters more than it would in a raid game.
- **Trade and give.** Give to villagers works and refuses politely (Bell: "Lovely! Not mine, though. Keep it safe."). The dead-letter drawer works as a dead-drop. I could not test a player-to-player hand-over; nobody was awake. Wanted: a hand-over that survives the other person being asleep, with their name on it.
- **Guild or party.** None, and none wanted. A household of three that the Ledger writes as one line would be plenty.
- **Quest log.** `what now` reads the open threads; it emptied after Bell and Mott. Good shape, too short. Wanted: my own loose ends listed there (letters filed, things in the drawer, a room grown), so that the log is also a record of what I did.
- **Map.** None. The Hill of Long Shadows and the balcony telescope stand in for it, and I preferred them. Not wanted.
- **Progress tracking.** Strong: the Book (4/172, per page, with the four minutes I found written out) and the Ledger of deeds. The key board should be the third tracker and is unreadable.
- **Leaderboards.** None, and none wanted. The Ledger is the right leaderboard: names, no numbers.
- **Chat channels.** Room-local speech only, private replies from villagers, no whisper, no global. Fits. Mail should cover the rest and nearly does.
- **Emotes.** A handful of authored verbs (`sit`, `wait`, `pet`, `ring`) that each return a written line; `sit on the weathered bench` was better than any `/sit` I have used. No free emote, and I did not miss it.

Of the missing pieces, the ones I actually wanted in this game, in order: an inbox with Fen; last-seen for the two people I care about; a readable key board; a way to address a left thing to a person. Not wanted: leaderboards, party, global chat, a map, free emotes.

## Would I log in tomorrow

Yes, once, and the second login decides it. I would come back to see whether Wren and Vex found the letters, the brass hand and the bookmark, and whether the village can tell me so. If the drawer is empty and nobody says who emptied it, if Fen greets me with the same brisk nod and the canned "the letters" line, then the world has no memory of the only thing I did that was for someone else, and I would drift.

The daily loop this village is built for, as far as I can see it: a few minutes to find (Mott said "a few for each dreamer each day"; I found four, all on one page, which a collector will notice), a letter to read or one to write, one guest to send home, ideally with someone, and a line in the Ledger for it. That is a fine coffee break. It needs the reading half of the mail and the villagers' memory to work.

Three improvements, in my own words.

1. **Answer the question before the topic.** When a sentence to a villager is a question about people or events and merely contains a topic word, answer the question; offer the topic afterwards or not at all. Five of my real questions tonight were swallowed by a passing noun, and it made every villager feel like a kiosk.
2. **Sign the traces.** Every grown room, every husk, every thing left in a drawer, every letter filed, should carry a name and a time the next dreamer can read, and the key board should show its chalk. The world already does this for the Ledger and for my own planting; do it for everyone, retroactively if possible. For a player arriving after friends, the signed trace is the whole game.
3. **Give villagers a memory of dreamers, not only deeds.** When a villager cannot know where an absent player is, say so honestly ("I have not seen them, but their names are in the blue book") instead of inventing a room. When a dreamer returns, open with the last thing they said or left. Fen telling me "two letters and a brass hand wait in my drawer for your friends, and nothing yet for you" would have been worth more than any improvised sentence I heard tonight.
