Persona: teenage expert gamer, plays fast, clicks everything, competes, pranks gently, quits when bored. Played alongside a second dreamer, **Wren** (the writer), who started in the same room at the same time.

95 commands (over the 90 budget; I kept going because the nap arc and the treehouse were good), about 25 minutes of wall time, dusk came while I played (rooms flipped from "time stands still here" to "day 1, dusk" the moment I opened the clock case).

## Rubric

1. **Surprise: 4.** Bell caught me stealing the lantern and named it: "Borrowing Pollen? She'll want to be home for dusk. She sulks if she's lit last." The nap "curls up small, and is simply gone, the way a nap is gone when you wake." A new dreamseed was growing inside the treehouse I had just planted. Tock's purr "keeps perfect time." On the other side, the world refusing my arcade and refusing to let me eat the fruit were surprises I did not enjoy.
2. **Consequence: 3.** The Ledger of Returned Hours recorded both deeds. Tace went from wistful to gladdened. Bell greeted me as "Tace's new keeper! I can hear your clock from here." Every room's clock started once I opened the case. But Bell said "Pollen burns proud" with Pollen in my treehouse, the brass hand I left for Wren sat untouched, and nothing I did could touch the other player mechanically.
3. **Being remembered: 3.** The Ledger has my name twice. Wren remembered my name and the cog. Bell knew who I was on the second visit. But nobody remembered the lantern after the moment it happened, and there is no mark of "Vex was here" except two rooms that do not say I grew them.
4. **Reason to return: 3.** 2/172 across twelve named pages is completionist bait, and the highest shelf and the folded thing are open loops. Against it: I saw exactly one stray minute on the ground in about fifteen rooms, there is no score to beat and no timer, and once the two arcs were done the map had two rooms of pure scenery in a row.

## Defects

Each is: the command, what happened, what I expected.

1. `talk to Wren: race you to the end of this thing` (and again with `talk to Wren: i made this whole room...`): "Wren is dozing, far off in a dream of their own, and doesn't stir." Then, in the same output, Wren answered me. She had moved rooms seconds before. Expected: no dozing note for a player who is clearly awake and playing.
2. `open clock case` while carrying the case key: "The clock case is locked; its small brass lock won't give without the right key." Expected: the key in my hand to count, or a nudge toward `use key on clock case`.
3. `take brass hand` and `examine paper weight` in the Bowl of Turned Time: "You don't see the brass hand here." The room listed "polished brass hand" and "folded paper weight". Elsewhere `take gear` and `take cog` matched partial names fine. Expected: partial names to work in grown rooms too.
4. `take dreamseed` in the Waiting Bell Loft: "Which dreamseed do you mean: dreamseed or spent dreamseed?" Typing `dreamseed` examined it instead of answering; `take new dreamseed` failed. Only `click take dreamseed` worked. Expected: the exact name "dreamseed" to win over "spent dreamseed", or the question to accept an answer.
5. `ring bell` in the Waiting Bell Loft: "There's no bell here to ring. The only one you know of sits on the counter of the Little Post Office, up Lamplight Lane." A "polished brass bell" is on the table and its examine says "to be rung when a friend arrives with the right word." The game built my room around a bell that does nothing. Expected: my bell to ring, at least once, at least when Wren walks in.
6. `give warm brass cog to Wren`: "Wren looks at the warm brass cog a moment, and leaves it with you." A player refused my gift in an NPC voice. Expected: to hand a friend something directly. Workaround: drop it and tell her; she took it.
7. `ask Bell "the lanterns"` after dusk with Pollen hidden in my treehouse: "Old Blue burns steady, Pollen burns proud." Bell reacted to the theft but not to the absence. Expected: "Pollen's still not home" or some worry, since Bell warned me about exactly this.
8. `plant dreamseed: a skate bowl where the ramps are giant clock hands and you can grind the minute hand`: got "The Bowl of Turned Time", a stone that "curves upward like a minute hand", three sentences about the weight of the hour lifting, and three objects: a paper weight I cannot read, a brass hand with no use, a spent seed. 9. `plant dreamseed: an arcade where every machine runs on stray minutes and the high score board is carved in moss`: "The seed turns that vision over and lets it go; nothing of that kind grows in this dream. Try another." Fine that it has taste, but the refusal gives no clue what kind does grow.

11. `read folded paper weight`: "You can't read the folded paper weight." Its examine says it holds "a small stack of crumpled notes that whisper of hours long past." Expected: readable.
12. `take evening fruit`, `eat evening fruit`: can't take; eat says "far too lovely to eat." The Orchard of Evenings has one thing in it and nothing to do with it. Same with `take littlest pendulum` in the garden. Two rooms of scenery back to back. Expected: something to do, or a reason to come back.
13. Chained moves (`north. west. up`, `down. down. east`, `west. north`): only the first step and the final room print. I walked through the well-court twice without seeing whether the brass hand was still there. Expected: a one-line "you pass through X" per step, with what is on the ground.
14. Bell's greeting after `down. down. east`: "That's Pollen, that's Old Blue, and the little bright one is Thimble," while I was holding Pollen. Minor tone slip.
15. `say Wren watch this. im gonna plant a seed`: she had left one line earlier. There is no way to find another dreamer or reach them across rooms. I spent a lot of my session chasing her by "Wren heads north" lines. Expected: "where is Wren", or a whisper, or a way to leave a note.
16. Stray minutes: 0/172 for my first 48 commands; the first was handed to me by Mott, the second was on the cellar floor. One in the wild in about fifteen rooms. For a counter that prints in every snapshot, that reads as a broken drop rate. Expected: a few glinting somewhere I walk on day one.
17. `read Ledger of Returned Hours`: "The great clock was mended by Wren and Vex together." I found the gear, carried it, earned the key, opened the case; Wren was in the loft. Co-credit for a spectator. Expected: the deed to say who did what, or a per-dreamer tally.
18. `wind small clock` in the square: a lovely line, no effect on a nap that "needs something slow to fall asleep to." A ticking clock in hand plus that clue made the failure feel arbitrary; the second clock was the answer. Expected: a nudge ("slower than that").
19. `what now`: one line, about the first winding. Fine, but after the nap I had two dangling mysteries and no list. Expected: it to name the open loops (the shelf, the folded thing).

## Would I play tomorrow, honestly

**Where boredom set in.** First dip at commands 27 to 34, standing in the Bowl of Turned Time: my skate bowl was a poem with a paperweight I could not read and a brass hand with no use. I nearly bailed. The `what now` line and Wren pointing me at the nap pulled me back. Second dip at commands 78 to 82, the garden and orchard: could not take the pendulum, could not take or eat the fruit, Quill's answers had nothing to do. If Wren had not been in the world and the cellar had not had a stray minute on the floor, I would have quit at 82. I went to 95 because the cellar had a mystery shelf and a friend to leave a seed for.

**What would bring me back.** Whether Wren planted my seed and what grew. The jar on the highest shelf. Mott's folded thing. My treehouse. 2/172 with twelve named pages, if day two actually has minutes on the ground. Honest answer: a coin flip for a coffee break. Yes for one more run to check the seed and the shelf; no if day two looks like the garden and orchard.

**Three things that would make this more fun for a gamer.**

1. A visible per-dreamer tally and a clock on deeds. Minutes found, rooms grown, hours sent home, and "the clock was mended in 19 commands by Vex." The Ledger already exists; make it keep score and say who did what. Then racing Wren is a game instead of a vibe.
2. Let players actually interact. Give a thing to a friend, whisper or find them across rooms, ring the bell I built when they show up, leave a note. Right now the only two-player verbs that worked were `say` in the same room and dropping things on the floor.
3. More on the ground and more that does something. A stray minute glinting every few rooms, not one in fifteen. A readable paperweight, a takeable pendulum, a fruit that does anything. And when a seed refuses my vision, tell me what kind of thing grows here so the next one lands.
