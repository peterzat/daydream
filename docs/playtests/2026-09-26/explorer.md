# Playtest: The Explorer (Juniper)

Date: 2026-09-26, 17:45 to 18:01 Pacific (00:45 to 01:01 UTC), live server, blind.
Character: Juniper, "a tall person in a rain-dark wool coat, a map case slung across their back, ink on their fingers".
Other dreamers present: Marlow, Vesper, Oona.

## Session summary

About 150 inputs in 16 minutes of wall time, roughly 40 of them movement. That is over the brief's 50 to 90; the explorer persona walks a lot, and many free-text tries were one-line probes of what the world would answer.

Where I went (18 rooms, every listed exit, plus tried `down` in the cellar and at the well and `east` off the hill): the Clocktower, the Clockmaker's Loft, the Winding Balcony, the Hour Cellar, the Lantern Square, the Old Workshop, the Case of Mended Ticks (a room that grew north of the Clocktower during my session), the Mossy Well-Court, the Pendulum Garden, the Orchard of Evenings, Lamplight Lane, the Little Post Office, the Waiting House, the Lamp House, the Lantern Bridge, the Slow River's Edge, the Dusk Road, and the Hill of Long Shadows.

What I did and the threads I found:

- The stopped clock. The repair ledger explained the missing escapement gear. While I was in the cellar, Marlow (another player) returned it. When I came up, every room's header had flipped from "(time stands still here)" to "(day 1, dusk)", a dreamseed and a warm brass cog lay on the Clocktower floor, and the Ledger of Returned Hours now read "The great clock was mended by Marlow, and time began again in the village." A few minutes later the Clocktower had a new north exit into a grown room.
- Pim's Nap. A lost birthday nap appeared in the square. Bell pointed me to Mott's hush; I fetched it and gave it over ("It is quieter now, but it still cannot quite sleep. It needs something slow to fall asleep to."). I never found the "something slow" myself. Later I found the nap asleep in the Waiting House, and the ledger credited me along with the other three players.
- The first winding. Tace gave me a small resting clock "by old custom"; winding it was a quiet, good moment, and Tace sent me to Bell and to Mott, who gave me the Book of Stray Minutes.
- Stray minutes. I found two on my own (the Lamp House, the river's edge) and got a third from Mott. The book shows 3/172 across twelve chapters.
- The Wend mystery, which is what would bring me back: a little brass clock marked "W" stopped at eleven on Tace's top shelf ("'Not that one, friend,' Tace says quietly" when I tried to wind it), a single jar on the Hour Cellar's highest shelf that no ladder reaches, Mott's folded thing under his thumb, Bell glancing east, a Lamp House where "nothing in the room faces east," and a hill bench that faces a sky that "never quite decides to become morning while anyone is watching." Tock the cat sits on the balcony "at dawn."

## Rubric

**Surprise: 4/5.** The village changed under my feet: time started while I was in a cellar, and a room that did not exist three minutes earlier appeared off the Clocktower. Small authored details surprised me as well: the jar labels ("'Tuesday, the long one', 'the hour before the recital', 'rain, unspent'"), the well that "is being polite," and a nap that explains itself as "Pim was seven, and there was cake and a red kite and too much singing, so Pim wouldn't sleep, and I got lost." It falls short of 5 because the second half of the session was repetitive scaffolding and parser refusals.

**Consequence: 4/5.** My hush visibly changed the nap (restless to drowsy, a new "Still too loud... Somewhere." line), the nap later moved house and fell asleep, and the Ledger of Returned Hours wrote the outcome down with my name in it. The clock case examine text changed once the clock was mended ("The door stands open, and behind the glass the pendulum sways bright and sure"). It is not a 5 because the decisive step on the nap happened elsewhere, by others, and I only learned about it by stumbling into the Waiting House.

**Being remembered: 3/5.** The ledger remembered me: "Day 1: Pim's nap chose to stay at the Waiting House, tucked up by Juniper, Vesper, Oona and Marlow." Oona, a player, greeted me by name. No NPC ever used my name. Bell said "Hello again!" on what was, as far as I could tell, our first real conversation. When I asked Mott directly "Do you remember me?", his late second reply was "Of course, little one. The dust and I remember every step you take." That line is warm but generic, and "little one" does not fit a tall person in a wool coat. Linden kept saying "there's a cup with your name on it somewhere" after she had already poured me one.

**Reason to return: 4/5.** Yes. I would come back tomorrow for a coffee break to go up to the balcony at dawn with Tock, to see whether the W clock or the high jar ever becomes reachable, and to reread the ledger for what other players did overnight. The Wend thread is the strongest hook in the game. What would wear me down over a week: the same entrance lines on every room entry, and the "You think to yourself... The daydream answers softly" wall whenever I try a natural action.

## Defects

1. **My speech was eaten and replaced with a non-sequitur.** Command: `say Hi Oona! I'm Juniper, a mapmaker of sorts. I'm going up to see the loft.` Result: "Juniper doesn't have much to say just now." No line was spoken. Expected: `Juniper says: "Hi Oona! ..."`. The game treated my `say` as talking to myself.
2. **Say truncated, and a player treated as an NPC.** Command: `say Hello, fellow dreamers. Anyone know where this clock's gear went?` Result: `Juniper says: "Hello, fellow dreamers"` then "Oona doesn't have much to say just now." The question was dropped, and Oona (a player) was "talked to." Expected: the whole line spoken to the room.
3. **Saying something moved me.** Command: `say I'm off to see the loft`. Result: the line was spoken and then I was walked up to the Clockmaker's Loft, with Tace (who was upstairs) answering "'Go up, if you like,' Tace says" before the scene changed. Expected: speech only. I liked where it put me, but the input was a `say` and should not move me.
4. **Other players' actions shown to me in the second person.** In the loft, the meanwhile block read "You take the brass pendulum." I took nothing, and `inventory` said "You're carrying nothing." In the grown room, `examine bundle of dried gears` ended with "You think to yourself: "eat the pocket watch". The daydream answers softly." I never typed that. Someone else's private unparsed input was broadcast to me as my own thought. Expected: other players' actions in the third person with their name, and fallbacks kept private to the typist.
5. **A talk that returned nothing.** Command: `talk to Tace: Bell says you know about lullaby clocks. Pim's Nap is in the square and needs something slow to fall asleep to.` Result: 4.1 seconds, then no output at all. Also `talk to Marlow` after Marlow had left printed only a meanwhile line and no reply or "Marlow isn't here." Expected: a reply, or a clear failure line.
6. **The nap's second need was a dead end for me.** Bell said "Tace could tell you about lullaby clocks." `ask Tace about lullaby clocks` returned an unrelated great-clock line ("It ticks. I keep checking, the way you check a sleeping child is breathing."). A retry got "I do not know if such a clock is waiting in the loft for you." Bell told Oona "A hushed nap would fall asleep to their slow tick" (the resting clocks), but `take resting clocks` said "You can't take the resting clocks," and `give small clock to Pim's Nap` said "It wants something quieter than that." I never learned what the answer was. Expected: at least one hint that lands, or a topic chip for the lullaby thread.
7. **Parser splits on commas and "and".** `take the jar labeled rain, unspent` returned "You don't see the jar labeled rain here." and "You don't see the unspent here." `drop a pebble into the well and listen` returned "You don't see the pebble into the well here." and "You don't see the listen here." The replies echo my words back as if they were objects, which reads as broken.
8. **Described details that do not exist when touched.** `examine the clock painted with forget-me-nots` (named in the resting clocks' own description) got "You don't see the clock painted with forget-me-nots here." `read one of the small cards` (grown room) got "You don't see the one of the small cards here." `examine the clocks on the shelves` got the same. `read a letter from the pigeonhole marked TO WHOEVER FINDS THIS` got the same. `look out the west window` (Lamp House) and `look through the telescope toward the west` fell back to the room description. For an explorer, a prose detail that refuses to exist is the most deflating response. Expected: a short flavor line, even if the thing cannot be taken.
9. **Natural actions hit a wall, inconsistently.** `ring the brass bell` on a post office counter returned "You can't use the brass bell." `wait`, `sit on the bench and face east`, `climb one of the crooked trees`, `watch for the small bright thing under the water`, and `stand still in the meadow and wait for the falling lights` all returned "You think to yourself: "..." The daydream answers softly." The last one came right after Sorrel told me "You stand still in the right place, and they come to you." Meanwhile `scratch Tock behind the ears`, `hum a slow lullaby to Pim's Nap`, and `listen to the drip down in the well` each got a lovely improvised line. The inconsistency is what stings: I cannot predict which gestures the world will meet.
10. **Verbatim repeated lines.** "Tace looks up from a bench of small clocks, sets down a fine brass tool, and offers a tired, kind nod." appeared at least five times, including after `examine small clock` and `wind small clock` when I had not moved. "Between one tick and the next, a small grey cat is simply there, washing one white paw as if it had always been." appeared on every loft visit; by the third, the magic is gone. "Bell is up a small ladder coaxing a lantern alight, and lifts a sooty hand in greeting." appeared on every square entry, including right after we had just talked, and was re-printed in the meanwhile after I gave the hush. Linden's "a cup with your name on it somewhere" line came again after she poured the cup. Tace's "It is a pleasure to meet you, Oona. Please, do not mind the ticking; it is just the little clocks finding their own rhythm." appeared twice, nearly word for word.
11. **Delayed second replies.** `talk to Sorrel: it's six o'clock. Are the hours falling tonight? Can I help you catch one?` got a reply at 6.3 s, then a second, different reply ("You may hold the line if your hands are steady enough.") arrived after I had walked two rooms away, offering something I could no longer do. Same with Mott: a second reply ("Of course, little one. The dust and I remember every step you take.") arrived after I left the workshop. Expected: one reply, delivered while I am still there.
12. **Replies that ignore the question.** `talk to Umber. I'm a mapmaker. Is there anything under this cellar, or is this the bottom of the village?` got "Glad to meet you. Take a seat; the tea is steeping just right. Keep your feet off the dusty floorboards." `talk to Bell. Hello! Tace sent me, I just wound my first clock.` got "Hello again! Pollen glows just a little brighter..." with no reaction to the clock, even though Tace had said "Bell will want to meet you." "Pollen" was unexplained until I asked; it turns out to be a lantern. The same input also made me wind my clock again ("You wind your clock and hold it to your ear"), an action I described in the past tense and did not ask for.
13. **Tace sounds like it does not know its own shelf.** `talk to Tace about the little brass clock with the W on it` got "I do not know what belongs on its face. It is not among the pieces I have mended, nor the things in Mott's tin. I cannot say where it came from." The clock sits on Tace's top shelf, and Tace stopped me winding it. If this is meant as withholding, it reads as ignorance rather than a secret. The chip answer ("Some shelves are too high for me") does the withholding well; the free-text answer undercuts it.
14. **Continuity slips.** Mott said "Here's your first" as he handed me my third stray minute. The Clocktower's room text never mentions the new north exit ("...and the square is east") while Ways lists north. The clock case says "its little brass door held by a delicate lock. The door stands open" in one breath. Mott's hush line ("he lifts out something grey and soft...") never says he hands it over; I only found it in my inventory with `look`.
15. **Grammar in examine output.** "You examine the Mott's tin", "You examine the hush: a hush: a small grey quiet", "You examine the stray minute: a stray minute: a tiny flicker of light", and "You don't see the one of the small cards here."
16. **The grown room is thin and slightly garbled.** "The Case of Mended Ticks" describes cards and clocks you cannot examine, and its one object, a "bundle of dried gears," is "used to patch the broken springs of clocks that refused to stop ticking," which reads backwards. "Dried gears" is an odd image. Giving it to Quill, the only gardener, got it handed straight back.
17. **Dusk at 18:00 passed unmarked.** The village flipped to "(day 1, dusk)" at about 17:49, when the clock was mended, so there was no dusk transition for me to see. At 18:00 I was on the Dusk Road and the hill. The room text says lights "drift down here like slow snow" at dusk, but nothing arrived or fell, and no event marked the hour. For a player told that dusk matters, the moment was invisible.
18. **Slow free-text replies.** Topic chips answered in about 1.3 s. Free-text talk took 5.8 s (Tace), 6.8 s (Umber), 7.7 s (Tace), 8.1 s (Quill), 6.3 s (Sorrel), and 7.0 s (Mott). That is tolerable for a cozy game, but 8 s feels like the game froze, especially since nothing tells you a reply is coming.
19. **The telescope does nothing.** It is the balcony's one object, and pointed east where "the sky is always about to decide something." It can be examined but not looked through or turned, and the attempt falls back to the room description.

## Three best moments

1. Coming up from the cellar to find the whole village changed by another player: the header gone from "(time stands still here)" to "(day 1, dusk)", then reading "The great clock was mended by Marlow, and time began again in the village," then, at the very end, "Day 1: Pim's nap chose to stay at the Waiting House, tucked up by Juniper, Vesper, Oona and Marlow." Seeing a shared world write itself down, with my name in it, is the thing this game does that nothing else I have played does.
2. The Lamp House: a narrow bed facing the west window, "nothing in the room faces east," and a stray minute turning in the air. Taking it: "the dew minute. It was footprints across a lawn gone silver with dew, the first of the day." A mystery and a small gift in one room, found by following my nose up a stair.
3. `listen to the drip down in the well`: "You listen at the well. The echo of your own breath comes back softer, as if the well is being polite." Along with the jar labels in the Hour Cellar and Pim's Nap ("'Not sleepy,' it whispers. 'Very sleepy.'"), this is the writing at its best: small, specific, and kind.
