# Playtest: The Chatterbox (Oona)

Persona: plays for the people. Talks to everyone in full sentences, goes off-script on purpose, and tests whether characters remember her.

Character: Oona, "a round-faced woman in a knitted cardigan full of pockets, with a laugh that carries."

Session: 2026-09-27 00:45 to 01:00 UTC (17:45 to 18:00 Pacific, so I crossed into dusk mid-session). About 95 inputs to the game, plus a few look and book calls. Three other playtesters (Marlow, Juniper, Vesper) were in the world with me.

## Session summary

I woke in the Clocktower with the other three dreamers, read the repair ledger (the escapement gear rolled south toward the well-court; bring it to Tace for the case-key) and the empty Ledger of Returned Hours. I climbed to the Clockmaker's Loft and spent a long time with Tace, then went up to the Winding Balcony and met Tock, a grey cat who blinks in and out of existence. While I was on the balcony, Marlow mended the great clock: when I came down, the status line had changed from "(time stands still here)" to "(day 1, dusk)", Tace was "gladdened", and a "first winding" topic had appeared. Tace gave me a small clock of my own and I wound it.

In the Lantern Square I met Bell (lamplighter) and a new dusk guest, Pim's Nap, a birthday nap too excited to be taken. I visited Mott in the Old Workshop (he gave me my Book of Stray Minutes and my first minute), Fen in the Little Post Office, and Linden in the Waiting House, who poured me tea. Clicking Linden's "keeping the nap here" topic ended the nap's thread: the nap appeared in the littlest armchair and I got a second stray minute. I peeked into Bell's bare room (the Lamp House), went back to Bell and Tace to test their memory of me, then went down to the Hour Cellar to meet Umber. I ended by reading the Ledger of Returned Hours, which now credited all four of us.

Threads I found: the missing escapement gear (solved by Marlow, not me); Pim's Nap (kept at the Waiting House); Mott's small folded thing he keeps his thumb over; the single jar on Umber's highest shelf that "no ladder in the cellar is tall enough" to reach; Bell's avoidance of dawns ("Dawns are for other people"); Wend, the clockmaker who is gone.

## Rubric

1. **Surprise: 4/5.** Tock was a real delight ("Tock vanishes. For one whole tick there is only a small warm dent where it was sitting, and then Tock is there again, washing a paw."). The clock starting mid-session and dusk bringing a guest changed the whole village while I was chatting on a balcony. Linden chalked my name on a saucer, and later Umber riffed on it.
2. **Consequence: 3/5.** The Ledger recorded "Day 1: Pim's nap chose to stay at the Waiting House, tucked up by Juniper, Vesper, Oona and Marlow," and Bell later said "You just helped Pim's Nap settle in at the Waiting House, right?" That felt good. But my one real deed was unearned (one topic click in another room finished a thread Juniper had done the work on). Tace had never heard of it ("Tace has not heard of Pim's nap"), and Fen's promise to send my letter led nowhere.
3. **Being remembered: 2/5.** Bell, Fen and Linden used my name, but that seems to come from the name I signed in with, not from memory. Tace, the character I spent the most time with, never recalled my name or my bees. First "I do not know your name, friend, nor do I know of bees or knitting" (seconds after I told them both), then later "Names are just labels for the hands that wind the clock." Bell remembered a deed but got the order wrong.
4. **Reason to return: 3/5.** Yes, probably, for a coffee break: the authored lines are the best writing in the game, dusk brings someone new, and the Book of Stray Minutes is a gentle reason to wander. But as a chatterbox I found free-form talk a coin flip. Characters dodge personal questions with "I do not know", answer other players' questions at me, and repeat themselves. I would come back for the place more than for the conversations.

## Defects

1. **Multi-sentence input is split at every period, and the tail gets misparsed or dropped.**
   - `say Juniper, Vesper, hello! I'm Oona. Have either of you met anyone who lives here yet?` printed `Oona says: "Juniper, Vesper, hello! I'm Oona"` then `Ask what?`. Expected: the whole line said aloud.
   - `say Hey Vesper! The ledger says someone called Tace is up in the loft. I'm going to go say hello, come along if you like` printed two separate says, and the second lost "I'm going to go": `Oona says: "hello, come along if you like"`.
   - `say Juniper, was that you who gave the little nap a hush? It's so much calmer now. Bell says it needs something slow to fall asleep to` said the first two sentences, and the last one vanished without any message.
   - The same split applies to `talk to X: ...`. Each sentence becomes its own talk, so replies arrive out of order in later "meanwhile" blocks, and some sentences get non sequitur answers (see 8).
2. **"I keep bees back home" is parsed as an inventory command.** It happened twice, with Mott (`talk to Mott: Hello Mott, I'm Oona. I keep bees back home, ...`) and Fen (`talk to Fen: Hello! I'm Oona, new in the village. I keep bees back home. ...`). Both times the game printed `You're carrying: small clock.` in the middle of the conversation. Expected: part of what I said to the character.
3. **My first `say` produced no output at all.** `say Hello, everyone! I'm Oona. Is this where the clock lives?` returned nothing, and a follow-up look showed no trace of it. Expected: `Oona says: ...`.
4. **Players cannot be talked to, and the refusal is confusing.** `talk to Marlow` (Marlow was listed under "Here with you") returned "You don't see that here." `examine Marlow` returned "You don't see the Marlow here." When other players tried to talk to me, the room saw "Oona doesn't have much to say just now." That reads like the game judging me as a character. Expected: player-to-player talk routed as speech, or a clear hint to use `say`.
5. **Other players' private results show up in my feed in second person.** My "meanwhile" blocks contained "You take the brass pendulum." (I never took it, and my inventory stayed empty), "You're carrying nothing.", "You think to yourself: "pet tock". The daydream answers softly.", "You turn the little key..." (printed twice, once for someone else and once for me), and Mott's first-minute line "He tips something bright out of the tin into your palm" a second time, for another player. Expected: third person ("Vesper takes the brass pendulum") or not shown at all.
6. **NPC replies have no addressee, so a shared room becomes crosstalk.** With four of us talking to Tace, I could not tell which line was meant for me. `talk to Tace about what they honestly think of Mott` returned "I do not know of such things. There are no phones here, only the hands of clocks that tell us when the light is fading." The real answer about Mott arrived in the next call's meanwhile. `talk to Tace: you look happier than before!...` returned Tace's first-winding speech ("They set it in your hands, warm from the shelf") but gave me nothing. The clock only arrived when I clicked the "the first winding" chip. Expected: replies clearly addressed ("Tace tells Vesper...").
7. **The "I do not know" guard shows through as robotic denial.** Tace: "I do not know your name, friend, nor do I know of bees or knitting," right after I gave both. Tace: "I do not know what I had for breakfast, friend." Tace: "I do not know what you speak of," right after Tace had brought up Wend. Mott: "Don't know, and I won't pretend I do." Bell: "Oh, I don't know what Tace was like before the clock stopped," from a neighbor of many years. Expected: in-character deflection (Umber does this well with tea) instead of a flat denial.
8. **An AI-denial line leaked into a topic reply.** `ask Oona Tace "Wend"` returned the lovely authored Wend line, followed by "I am no model of steel, just a soul who listens to the ticking." Earlier the meanwhile carried "I am not a thing of metal and gears like the little ones in my care, but a soul who listens to them." Someone probably asked Tace if they were a robot or an AI, and the answer is bleeding into my conversation. Expected: no appended line on a chip, and no fourth-wall material.
9. **Tace refers to themself in the third person.** "Tace remembers your name, friend, though it is one of the many hands..." and "No, friend, Tace has not heard of Pim's nap, though they wonder if it might be resting in the Waiting House."
10. **Lines repeat verbatim.**
    - "The clocks are not junk, they are just waiting for a hand to turn them gently. And I am not old, only slow as a winter day." (Tace, twice in a row, to another player)
    - "Tace takes the spectacles off, cleans them slowly on the apron, and puts them back. 'Some shelves are too high for me,' they say, and that is all." (twice)
    - Mott: "I gave the hush to Pim for that restless nap, so the boy might finally rest," then again as his reply to my goodbye.
    - Umber: "Remember to taste the tea..." in two consecutive replies.
    - Linden's arrival line "says there's a cup with your name on it somewhere" came back after she had already poured it.
11. **Mott contradicts himself.** `ask Mott about the hush for the little nap in the square` returned "The hush is safe in my tin. I gave it to Pim for that restless nap, so the boy might finally rest." It cannot be both in the tin and given away, and Pim is the boy, not the nap.
12. **Mott flattens his own mystery.** The authored line is "Some things you keep because you're not ready to know them." When I asked in my own words, the model said "That is mine alone. I don't know who gave it to me, and I cannot tell you more." That invents a fact that undercuts the subtext.
13. **A topic chip resolved a shared thread remotely and without effort.** `ask Oona Linden "keeping the nap here"` from the Waiting House, while the nap was in the square, triggered the ending. The nap teleported into the armchair, and I got a stray minute. Juniper had found and given the hush; I had only chatted. Bell had also pointed at a lullaby clock from Tace as another path, which is now presumably closed. Expected: the nap present, or at least the player who helped doing the tucking in.
14. **Invented capability and a punctuation glitch.** `talk to Fen: I'd like to write a letter to my sister back home...` returned "'That is first-class news, Oona. The lanterns are a lovely subject.'. 'I have plenty of good stationery for your sister, and I will make sure it gets to her by the time the dusk light fades.'" Nothing followed. The next reply asked "Just tell me who the letter is meant for," although I had already said my sister. Note the stray `.'.` too.
15. **Bell's memory is garbled.** `talk to Bell: I'm back! Do you remember me? What did I tell you I had just done when we met?` returned "Of course one! You just helped Pim's Nap settle in at the Waiting House, right? It was the very first thing we did when you arrived." "Of course one!" is broken, and what I had actually told Bell was that I had wound my first clock.
16. **The model's gloss undercut authored subtext.** Bell's chip line "Dawns are for other people. I've got dusks. Dusks are plenty." is quietly sad. The free-form reply just before it chirped "It is a lovely joke, isn't it? The dawn always waits for me in the Waiting House," which gives the ache away as a joke.
17. **Free-form questions sometimes return an unrelated authored line.** `ask Fen what she thinks of Bell, and whether Bell ever gets any letters` returned "To whoever finds this. Well, someone will. That's what I'm here for." `ask Linden how she came to run the Waiting House and whether she ever wanted to do anything else` returned a stock line about rooms for hours. Neither answered the question.
18. **Odd cat behavior in the feed:** "Tock smiles and gently sets the small clock back in your hands." A cat that smiles and hands me clocks breaks Tock's otherwise perfect characterization. It was likely a reply to another player, shown to me as "your hands".
19. **Parser misses.** `look through the telescope` printed the room description instead of the view. `examine Mott's tin` printed "You examine the Mott's tin".
20. **Pronoun drift for Mott.** Tace said "the very small folded thing they hide from us all," while every authored line calls Mott "he".
21. **Slow replies.** Free-form talk often took 7 to 9 seconds: Tock 7.2 s, Tace 7.6 s, Bell 8.1 s, Umber 8.5 s, Linden 8.5 s, Pim's Nap 8.6 s. Chips answered in about 1.3 s. At 8 seconds I started wondering whether my line had been lost, especially with replies arriving out of order.

## Three best moments

1. **Linden's tea, carried across three characters.** Linden poured "into a cup with Oona's name written in chalk on the saucer," described "a blend of the late summer blooms and the honey from the bees that never left the garden" (after I had mentioned my bees elsewhere), and called me "lamb". Later I told Umber that Linden had chalked my name, and Umber answered: "Linden has a steady hand, and a saucer that remembers well." This was the one stretch where the village felt like it was listening.
2. **The Ledger of Returned Hours.** It went from "No hour has been returned yet" to "The great clock was mended by Marlow, and time began again in the village. Day 1: Pim's nap chose to stay at the Waiting House, tucked up by Juniper, Vesper, Oona and Marlow." Seeing my name beside three strangers' in a book on a lectern is exactly the multiplayer warmth the game is reaching for.
3. **Tock, Pim's Nap, and the Lamp House.** Tock's "washes its white paw, which is the whole answer, and a thorough one." Pim's Nap's "Not sleepy. Very sleepy. Are you singing to me, you?" and its callback in the armchair: "Yes, I miss the red kite, but the hush is nice here." The Lamp House's quiet detail, "nothing in the room faces east", paid off Bell's line about dawns without anyone saying so.

## Conversation quality

**Chips versus free-form.** The ask-about chips are fast (about 1.3 s) and always return authored lines, and those lines are the best writing in the game. Free-form talk takes 3 to 9 seconds and ranges from lovely to broken. It is strongest when it riffs on a concrete detail I offered (tea, a saucer, a letter, a red kite). It is weakest on memory, on opinions of other villagers, and on personal off-script questions, where the "I do not know" reflex fires. It often falls back to an authored line that does not answer what I asked. In a crowded room the lack of an addressee makes free-form talk very hard to follow.

**Best exchanges (quoted exactly):**

- Me: `ask Fen whether she has ever written a letter of her own that she never sent`. Fen: "That is a question for the inside of a letter, not the front, and I do not write my own unsent words. But I will keep yours safe if you wish to send it." (Free-form, fully in voice. My favorite model-written line of the session.)
- Me: `talk to Bell: Hello Bell! I'm Oona, I just wound my first clock up in the loft and Tace said you'd want to meet me`. Bell: "Oona! Tace mentioned you, and I am glad to meet you. You wound your clock? That is wonderful news!"
- Me: `talk to Umber: I have had my tea, Linden chalked my name on the saucer...`. Umber: "Linden has a steady hand, and a saucer that remembers well."
- Me: `talk to Tace about what they honestly think of Mott` (the reply that arrived late). Tace: "I think of Mott as a quiet keeper of found things, though I do not know the very small folded thing they hide from us all."
- Me: `talk to Pim's Nap: are you comfy in your little armchair now? Do you miss Pim?`. Nap: "The chair is very soft. Yes, I miss the red kite, but the hush is nice here."
- Authored chip lines that earned their place: Fen on Umber, "She keeps the hours nobody spent, I keep the letters nobody sent. We don't compare notes. Much." Umber, "Every jar is somebody's later. Later mostly doesn't come. So here they are." Bell, "Dawns are for other people. I've got dusks. Dusks are plenty." Mott, "Some things you keep because you're not ready to know them."

**Worst exchanges (quoted exactly):**

- Me: `talk to Tace: Hello Tace! I'm Oona. I keep bees back home. How has your day been?`. Tace: "I do not know your name, friend, nor do I know of bees or knitting. But if you are here to mend the great clock, you are welcome."
- Me: `talk to Tace about what they honestly think of Mott`. Tace (first reply shown to me): "I do not know of such things. There are no phones here, only the hands of clocks that tell us when the light is fading."
- Me: `ask Tace what my name is`. Tace: "Names are just labels for the hands that wind the clock. Yours seems to fit the rhythm of the room."
- Me: `ask Oona Tace "Wend"`, answered with the authored line and then: "I am no model of steel, just a soul who listens to the ticking."
- Me: `talk to Tace: then what is my name, and what do I keep back home`. Tace: "A soft hello from you, is like a gentle tick that fills the quiet air. The great clock has begun to breathe again, and the day feels warm for once."
- Me: `ask Mott about the hush for the little nap in the square`. Mott: "The hush is safe in my tin. I gave it to Pim for that restless nap, so the boy might finally rest."
- Me: `talk to Bell: I'm back! Do you remember me?...`. Bell: "Of course one! You just helped Pim's Nap settle in at the Waiting House, right? It was the very first thing we did when you arrived."

**Summary judgment.** The authored voices are distinct and good: Tace's tired kindness, Bell's brightness over something sad, Mott's reticence, Fen's briskness, Umber's dry deflections, Linden's mothering. The model keeps those voices on the surface but not in substance. It does not hold what I told it, it answers with denials when it should deflect in character, and in a shared room it answers the wrong person. Tace in particular, the first and most important character, came off as the least alive in free conversation.
